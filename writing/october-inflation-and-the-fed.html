October Inflation Made December More Complicated

Jacob George · November 10, 2026

The inflation report on November 10 looked better than September’s at first glance. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, down from 0.6% the month before, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The problem was that economists surveyed by Reuters had expected a 0.3% increase. The numbers can improve from one month to the next and still make investors more worried about what the Fed will do. That’s what happened here.

Most of the slowdown came from energy. Energy prices rose 0.7% after jumping 5.2% in September, while core CPI, which leaves out food and energy, rose 0.3% for a third straight month. Shelter alone accounted for more than 30% of October’s monthly increase in the overall index. I’d be more convinced inflation was cooling if core prices had slowed with the headline number. The other wrinkle was that the 2025 government shutdown left the BLS without October 2025 data for most CPI series, so there was no usual year-over-year headline figure to put the monthly number in context.

The reaction was clearer in bonds than in stocks. Reuters reported that the two-year Treasury yield rose about 18 basis points to roughly 4.73%, while the S&P 500 fell 0.4%. Markets had already expected a December rate increase. After the report and comments from Fed Governor Christopher Waller, futures pointed to a higher chance of a larger half-point move. The two-year yield is where I’d look first to see that change in expectations, since it’s more closely tied to what investors think the Fed will do over the next several meetings.

What matters for stocks is how long rates might stay high. If I’m putting a value on a company whose best years are far in the future, a higher rate makes those future profits worth less today. The same company might also have to pay more to borrow. That gives me a reason to expect stocks with the highest expectations for future growth to be sensitive to the next inflation surprise. One monthly report can move bond yields in an afternoon. I’d want to see core prices slow before taking a softer headline number as a reason to expect the Fed to back off.

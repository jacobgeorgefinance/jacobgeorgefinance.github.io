What Jensen Huang’s Return Means for Nvidia

Jacob George · November 24, 2026

Nvidia announced on November 23 that its board had reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president. He also returned to the board. The stock went from $125.53 at Friday’s close to $165.05 on Monday, a jump of 31.5%. Reuters reported that the move added nearly $1 trillion in market value. Even after that rally, Nvidia was still about 5% below where it traded before Huang stepped down on November 12. The size of the move makes more sense when you look at how much the stock had lost first.

For Nvidia, the benefit I see is that big customers have a better idea of who will make the decisions behind the next generation of AI chips. A cloud company planning a data center has to commit money before it knows exactly how much computing demand it will have. It matters whether it trusts Nvidia’s product schedule enough to plan around it. I think investors saw Huang’s return as a reason to have more confidence in those plans. Nvidia had already reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue on November 17, up 103% from a year earlier. That was evidence of how much customers were already spending. Monday’s news changed who would lead the business from here.

The board’s reversal still leaves me with a question about how much of Nvidia’s value investors tie to one person. Reuters reported that the chair had previously ruled out rehiring Huang and that President Donald Trump pushed for his return. In its announcement, Nvidia said the board voted unanimously and had received input from senior U.S. government officials. I’d want to know what the board will do to prepare for the next change in leadership. If the uncertainty around one executive can move the stock this much in either direction, having a credible successor matters even when the company is growing fast.

The impact reached well beyond Nvidia. The Nasdaq Composite closed up 6.5% and the S&P 500 rose 4.7% on Monday. Some of that came from the weight Nvidia itself carries in major indexes. The rest of the rally reflected buying across the market, including other AI stocks. Investors were already looking for a rebound before the announcement, so I wouldn’t give Huang’s return credit for the entire move.

I expect the bounce to help sentiment toward companies that sell into the same data center buildout. A supplier can make a more convincing case for its own growth if customers are confident that big projects will go ahead. Whether that confidence lasts depends on what the customers do. The next signal I’d look for is whether big cloud companies keep their spending plans in place when they report earnings, especially with borrowing still expensive. Monday’s rally showed what investors think Huang is worth to Nvidia. Those spending decisions will tell us how far that confidence extends.

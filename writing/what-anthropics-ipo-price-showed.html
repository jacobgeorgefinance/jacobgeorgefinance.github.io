What Anthropic’s IPO Price Showed

Jacob George · November 20, 2026

Anthropic’s first day on the Nasdaq gave investors two very different prices for the same company. It sold shares in the offering at $83 apiece. On November 19, the stock opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17. That was still about 17% above the offer price, but someone who bought at the opening trade was down about 13% by the close. Whether it felt like a great day depended on when you got in.

The initial deal was worth $81.34 billion before the underwriters exercised their option to sell more shares. When the offering closed on November 20, Anthropic said the total had grown to about $93.5 billion, of which the company itself received about $91.6 billion before expenses. That’s the figure I’d focus on from Anthropic’s perspective. The first trading price gets the headlines, but the money the company raised gives it room to pay for computing capacity while it builds up its business. Investors buying from one another at $111.50 did not put additional cash into Anthropic.

For the rest of the market, the IPO offered a way to see what investors would pay for a frontier AI lab on a public exchange. A chipmaker earns money when it sells the hardware. Anthropic has to turn what it spends on that hardware into a service customers will keep paying for. Its prospectus disclosed about $900.9 billion in committed spending under purchase and lease agreements, including commitments stretching years into the future. That number is why I’d pay more attention to whether revenue can keep up with the cost of running Claude than to whether the stock opens above its offering price.

The broader market was already asking how much investors should pay for that growth. The Nasdaq fell 2.3% on Anthropic’s first trading day, even as the new listing finished above $83. I see the IPO as evidence that a leading AI company can still raise a huge amount of money in a weak market. The next test comes when investors can compare what Anthropic brings in each quarter with what it has already committed to spend. That’s where this IPO could change how the market values other AI companies.
